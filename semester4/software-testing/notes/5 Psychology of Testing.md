A tester:
-  Employs professional cynicism
-  Tries to prove that the solution does not work (they are detached from the ‘ownership’ and inherent protection Developers have for their code)

A Developer’s job is to prove the solutions does work

Testers see other and different defects to the author/developer.
Testers are unbiased
Testers do not make assumptions

Need to focus on effective communication
-  Constructive rather than confrontational
-  Present in a non-personal way
-  Empathy
-  Clarification

Testers should be pedantic and sceptical, but also impartial and constructive

## communications with developers

Communication problems may occur, particularly if testers are seen only as messengers of unwanted news about defects

Ways to improve relationships with others:
-  Collaboration, not battles
-  Neutral, objective, avoiding personalities
-  Understand how the other person feels and why they react as they do
-  Confirm that the other person has understood what you have said and vice versa.

## goal: locate defects

A successful test is one that locates a defect

If finding defects is the testers' aim:
-  positive motivation - finding defects is good
-  constructive - perceived as improving quality
-  testers create 'tough' tests, not easy ones
If we are effective at finding defects, and then can't find any, we can be confident the system works

## tester mindset

A destructive activity, requiring keen skills and tenacity
But also requires excellent communication skills
Destructive to products but constructive to authors and developers
Testers may benefit from a split personality?

Pedantic, skeptical, nit-picking to software
-  trust developers, but doubt the product nothing 'works', until you've tested it

Impartial, advisory, constructive to developers:
-  'tread lightly, as you tread on their dreams'
-  diplomatic, but firm: the defects were there to be found, you didn't put them there.

## independence

-  The mindset to be used while testing and reviewing is different to that used while analysing or developing
-  Independent testing may be carried out at any level of testing
-  A certain degree of independence (avoiding the author bias) is often more effective at finding defects and failures
-  Independence is not, however, a replacement for familiarity, and developers can efficiently find many defects in their own code

## degrees of independence

 the least independent to the most independent:
-  Developers used as testers
-  Separate permanent test team
-  Specialist testers such as usability testers,  performance testers, security testers etc.
-  Outsourced test team  (e.g. contractors, testing consultancy)


 the least independent to the most independent:
-  Test cases are designed by the person(s) writing the software
-  Test cases are designed by a person(s) from a different team
-  Test cases are designed by another person(s) in the same organisation
-  Test cases are designed by a person(s) from a different organisation
-  Test cases are not chosen by a person
